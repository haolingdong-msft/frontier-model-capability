Evaluating frontier models across capability, cost, response time, and task fit

How 11 frontier model families compare across five reasoning effort tiers—and how to pick the right model for complex engineering workflows.

Why choosing the right model for complex tasks is important

On a quick single-turn prompt, almost any frontier model feels fast and capable. On complex, long-running engineering tasks—multi-file refactoring, repository-wide API reviews, or iterative debugging—model choice directly determines whether your workflow finishes in minutes or burns hours and budget:

  • A model is a curve, not a single point: Stepping the same model from Low (1) to Max (5) reasoning effort can boost capability by +14 to +29 points, while multiplying per-task cost by 4x–19x and stretching single-turn latency from 7 seconds to 11+ minutes.
  • Single leaderboards hide cost and latency trade-offs: A configuration that tops an aggregate leaderboard at $10/task is often matched within a few points by a peer configuration costing under $0.60/task.
  • Long-running workflows compound every mismatch: Across dozens of agent turns and subagent calls, using a Max-effort flagship on routine steps creates massive latency bottlenecks, while using an under-powered model on architecture decisions causes costly retry loops.
